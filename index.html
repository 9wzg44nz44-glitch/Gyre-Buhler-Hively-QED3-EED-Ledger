Does this match Exodus published thrust?

Order-of-magnitude answer: No — not via quantum-tunneling electron momentum, and not via “all leak power → momentum at c.” Public secondary reports of Exodus thrust are roughly ~0.3–0.4 mN (earlier) to ~5–10 mN (later) — theirs / not verified here (ept.space; news summaries). Exact Exodus plate areas and Kapton (or other) thicknesses are not fully public; we use thin-film Kapton-class ranges (~7.5–125 μm, κ≈3.4) and cm²–scale electrodes as geometry brackets only.

1) Classical tunneling / field-emission recoil. Electrons that tunnel carry tiny momentum. Roughly Fe ≈ (I/e)·pe with pe=√(2mK). Even a generous leak I = 1 mA at K~50 eV gives only ~0.02 μN — about 104–106× too small vs claimed mN-class thrust. At microamps (more typical for a “good” dielectric), Fe is nano- to pico-newtons.

2) Optimistic bound: convert all electrical leak power to momentum at c (F ≤ P/c = IV/c). Example: 2 kV × 1 mA → 2 W → F ≤ ~7 nN (~0.007 μN). To reach 1 mN by P/c alone you would need ~300 kW of continuous leak power — not what a small stacked film capacitor draws after the supply is removed.

3) Size, area asymmetry, dielectric thickness/type. Thinner Kapton / higher V raises E = V/d and can raise tunneling probability exponentially, and larger area raises total leak current ~linearly with area. That can change I a lot — but Fe and P/c still scale only with I (or IV). Face-area asymmetry (η) can bias where leak or field is stronger; it does not multiply electron momentum by 106. So geometry helps the electrostatic-pressure story Exodus emphasizes; it does not make integrated tunneling-electron thrust “add up” to their claimed totals.

4) Different channel (not “tunneling electrons add up”). One-sided electrostatic pressure scale ½ε₀E²A on thin films can be large on paper; Exodus’s public patents stress asymmetric electrostatic pressure / dielectrics, not a measured ∫tunneling-electron recoil. Our SLW map (gSLW=ε₀CE from a continuity remainder) is a HYP bookkeeping idea — “not forbidden” if a real remainder exists, but not a quantitative match to published thrust from integrating tunneling alone. We do not fit σ to their thrust number. Warm skepticism. No breakthrough claim.

Back-of-the-envelope · check if numbers are in the ballpark

You do not need a PhD. Grab a phone calculator. Keep units straight. Goal: see whether a force you care about (Exodus claim, or the toy F_net on this page) could come from tunneling electrons — or whether the sim is only an educational cartoon.

Units cheat-sheet (dimensional analysis)

Force F is in newtons (N). 1 mN = 0.001 N. 1 μN = 0.000001 N.
Current I is in amperes (A). 1 mA = 0.001 A. 1 μA = 0.000001 A.
Voltage V in volts. Power P = I×V in watts (W).
Speed of light c ≈ 3×108 m/s. Electron charge e ≈ 1.6×10−19 C.

Step 1. Write the target force you are testing against.
Example claim ballpark: Fclaim ≈ 1 mN = 0.001 N (or 5–10 mN if you use the higher public secondary reports).
Step 2. Guess a leak / tunneling current after the supply is removed (or use a measured microammeter reading if you have one).
Conservative film capacitor: I ≈ 1 μA = 1e-6 A. Generous “leaky” case: I ≈ 1 mA = 1e-3 A.
Step 3A — electron recoil (classical particles).
Each electron carries tiny momentum. Rough napkin form:
F_e ≈ (I / e) × p_e with p_e ≈ √(2 m K).
Easier: at K~50 eV, order of magnitude is about F_e ~ (I in amperes) × 2×10−8 N (because 1 mA → ~0.02 μN).
So: I = 1e-3 → F_e ~ 2e-8 N = 0.02 μN.
Step 3B — absolute ceiling if all leak power becomes momentum at light speed.
Dimensions: power/speed = force. F ≤ P / c = (I × V) / c.
Example: I=0.001 A, V=2000 V → P=2 W → F ≤ 2 / 3e8 ≈ 7e-9 N ≈ 0.007 μN.
Step 4 — compare (the ballpark test).
Ratio R = Fclaim / Fyour formula.
If R ≈ 1–10: same order of magnitude (ballpark).
If R ≈ 100–1000: off by a lot; story needs another channel.
If R ≈ 104–109: not in the ballpark for that mechanism.
Example: 0.001 N / 2e-8 N ≈ 50,000 → tunneling-electron recoil is not in the ballpark for a 1 mN claim.
Step 5 — what about area and Kapton thickness?
Area A: total current often scales ~like A (more paths). That changes I, not the formula shape.
Thickness d: E = V/d; tunneling probability is wildly sensitive to d (exponential). Still, F_e and P/c only grow as I or I×V grow.
Asymmetry η: can change which face leaks more; it does not turn 0.02 μN into millinewtons by itself.
Step 6 — what about the toy F_net on this page?
The ON/OFF button shows an educational μN-scale number when tunneling is “on.” That number is not fitted to Exodus. Treat it as a cartoon of bookkeeping direction (F_net can be nonzero if a continuity remainder exists), not as a lab prediction. Ballpark test for the sim: if you turn tunneling OFF, F_net should go to ~0 (classical null). If ON stays huge while your napkin F_e and P/c are tiny, the sim is illustrating a HYP SLW channel — not proving tunneling electrons supply Exodus thrust.
Judgment rule: napkin math that stays within about a factor of ten of a claim is “in the ballpark.” Tunneling-electron recoil and P/c vs public mN-class Exodus reports fail that test by many orders. A different mechanism (e.g. asymmetric electrostatic pressure, or a hypothesized SLW field-momentum channel with unknown amplitudes) would need its own napkin formula — do not reuse F_e for that.
